# The History of the Islamic Society of RMIT (ISR)

A single consolidated history extracted from the research archive in
`research/isr-history/` (baseline 10 August 2026).

This document preserves the archive's core rule: nothing weak has been
discarded, and nothing uncertain has been promoted to fact. Recollection is
not converted into fact, inference is not converted into fact, and a
prayer-room establishment date is not converted into the Society's founding
date.

**Evidence grades used throughout**

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | Primary / official |
| B | Strong independent |
| C | Supporting historical |
| D | Research lead |
| I | Inference — never present as established fact |

---

## Executive summary

Muslim student life at RMIT has a documented history spanning decades.

- Dedicated Muslim prayer infrastructure appears to reach back to
  approximately the early 1990s.
- RMIT officially documented an active Muslim student community in **1997**.
- Biographical evidence identifies RMIT Islamic Society editorial activity
  spanning **1996–1999**.
- An external Islamic web index last modified in **February 1998** already
  linked directly to an RMIT-hosted page labelled "RMIT, Islamic Society".
- By **1999–2000** a biographical record identifies an Executive Committee
  and a Publication Wing.
- By **2003** academic research documents RMITIS maintaining a website,
  Islamic resources, a multimedia centre, lectures, conferences, video
  programs and Islamic Awareness Week.
- **2008–09** saw a major, well-documented prayer-room campaign.
- In **2024** RMITIS publicly rebranded as the Islamic Society of RMIT (ISR).

**The exact founding year remains unresolved.** A current ISR website claims
"Founded in 1993". That claim is a serious research lead, not a verified
founding date, and should not be published as established fact until primary
or sufficiently corroborated evidence is recovered.

## 1. Origins and the founding-date question

### Early-1990s dedicated prayer facility — Grade B

A later detailed study states that the dedicated RMIT Muslim prayer facility
demolished in late 2007 had existed for **almost fifteen years**, placing its
establishment at approximately 1992/93–94.

*Source:* Liam Ward and Katie Wood, "The RMIT University Muslim Prayer Room
Campaign 2008–2009" — https://archive.sa.org.au/mi/2/mi2wardwood.pdf

**What this supports:** dedicated Muslim prayer infrastructure at RMIT from
approximately the early 1990s.

**What it does not support:** it does not prove the Islamic Society was
founded in 1993.

*Follow-up:* locate original RMIT room-allocation/building records from
approximately 1991–1995.

### The "Founded in 1993" claim — Grade D pending provenance

A current Islamic Society of RMIT website states "Founded in 1993…"
(https://islamicsocietyofrmit.com.au/about). Targeted searches have not yet
located an independent contemporary source verifying 1993.

Possible explanations, none yet established:

1. Genuine institutional or oral knowledge not yet supported by a public source.
2. A founding year derived from an internal document not yet recovered.
3. An approximate date inferred from the early dedicated prayer room.
4. Website copy that introduced an unsupported date.

*Follow-up:* determine who authored the claim and what source was relied upon.

**DO NOT PUBLISH AS VERIFIED.**

## 2. 1996–2000 — Earliest organisational evidence

### Editorial activity, 1996–1999 — Grade D/C

A public professional biography records "Editor, RMIT Islamic Society" for
**Nasreen Badruddin**, within an RMIT study period of 1996–1999. This suggests
the Society had an editorial/publications function during the late 1990s.

*Source:* https://my.linkedin.com/in/nasreenb

Note: the record does not establish that she became editor in 1996 — only that
the role falls within that study period.

### Official RMIT reference, 1997 — Grade A

RMIT's 1997 Annual Report states that engineering student **Rini Akmeliawati**
"continues to be active in the RMIT Muslim student community." The report also
records that she completed a Bachelor of Engineering (Electrical) and won the
Patricia Guthrie Award.

*Source:* https://www.rmit.edu.au/assets/documents/about/Governance-and-management/annual-reports/1997-annual-report.pdf

This is official RMIT evidence of an identifiable Muslim student community by
1997. It does **not** identify that community by the formal name "RMIT Islamic
Society", and does not establish that Rini was an officer or member of a formal
society.

Her public academic profile gives her RMIT study period as **February 1993 –
March 1997**, making her an extremely high-value oral-history lead reaching
back to the disputed founding period.

### February 1998 web listing — Grade C (unusually strong dated web evidence)

A surviving international Islamic web-links index, last modified February 1998,
explicitly lists **"RMIT, Islamic Society"**, hyperlinked to:

```
http://minyos.its.rmit.edu.au/~zmuslims/
```

*Source:* Adi Kurniawan Islamic links index —
https://www.unhas.ac.id/rhiza/arsip/arsip-macam2/inislam.html

This establishes that a dedicated RMIT Islamic Society web presence was being
linked externally by February 1998. The RMIT-hosted page no longer responds and
is the archive's highest-priority digital recovery target.

### Executive Committee and Publication Wing, 1999–2000 — Grade D/C

A public professional biography records **Noor Hasbi Yusoff** as "Executive
Committee Member and Chairman, Publication Wing, RMIT Islamic Society
1999-2000" — a second independent late-1990s lead showing formal publication
infrastructure and executive structure.

*Source:* https://ae.linkedin.com/in/noorhasbiyusoff

### Working inference — Grade I (inference only)

Converging evidence — an editor role spanning 1996–1999, an official RMIT
Muslim student community reference in 1997, a dedicated society page indexed by
February 1998, and an Executive Committee with a Publication Wing in 1999–2000
— indicates the Society appears to have been a developed organisation by the
late 1990s, and likely predates the February 1998 web evidence.

**This must not be converted into a founding date.**

### Public presence, 2001 — Grade C

A contemporary publication identifies **Hashmat Moslih** as being from the
Islamic Society of RMIT during a Melbourne rally.

*Source:* Green Left — https://www.greenleft.org.au/2001/466/we-stand-solidarity

## 4. Salaam RMIT — the Muslim students handbook

### Student Union parliamentary record — Grade A (for the listing itself)

RMIT Student Union material submitted to an Australian Parliamentary inquiry
(mid-2000s) lists **"Salaam RMIT – Muslim students handbook"** among its
publications and services. The same submission refers to the RMIT Islamic
Society.

*Source:* https://www.aph.gov.au/~/media/wopapub/senate/committee/eet_ctte/completed_inquiries/2004_07/highed_unionfees/submissions/sub150_pdf.ashx

This does **not** yet prove the publication year, the authorship, or whether
RMITIS, the Student Union, or both together produced it.

### Independent academic corroboration — Grade B

Yumi Kishida's research (based on Australian university fieldwork 2007–08)
identifies "RMIT Muslim Students Handbook: Salaam RMIT" alongside Muslim
student guides at other universities, and describes such guides as covering
university organisations, prayer facilities, halal food, local mosques, halal
restaurants and grocers, medical services, clothing and Muslim organisations.

*Source:* https://kaken.nii.ac.jp/en/file/KAKENHI-PROJECT-19730518/19730518seika.pdf

Salaam RMIT may be one of the richest surviving snapshots of everyday Muslim
student life at RMIT before the 2007 prayer-room crisis. Recovering a complete
copy is a **P0 priority**, as is recovering the full 48-page Kishida report and
any appendices.

*Possible repositories:* RMIT Archives, RMIT Library, RUSU archives, former
RMITIS committees, former Student Union staff, individual alumni, the National
Library, State Library Victoria, and Kishida's research files.

### 2006 Muslim handbook lead — Grade D/C

A later opinion article states that RMIT produced a Muslim handbook in 2006
beginning with the phrase "In the name of Allah the Most Gracious, the Most
Merciful". The source is polemical commentary, not the original publication. It
may refer to Salaam RMIT, to a separate publication, or the phrase may simply
have been the opening line rather than a title. Preserve as a search lead only;
recover the original before any public use.

## 6. The 2007–2009 prayer-room campaign

The best-documented episode in ISR's history. The principal source is the Ward
& Wood campaign study (https://archive.sa.org.au/mi/2/mi2wardwood.pdf), whose
authors were themselves involved in the campaign — Grade B with perspective
qualification.

### The dispute — Grade B

The long-standing dedicated Muslim facility was demolished in late 2007.
Replacement male and female rooms were constructed within the Spiritual Centre
arrangement. RMITIS disputed their later multifaith/shared status and the
access arrangements.

### Campaign activity, Semester 1 2008 onward — Grade B

The campaign study records:

- a mass boycott;
- information stalls several times a week;
- approximately **1,100 petition signatures in one week**;
- hundreds of leaflets;
- campaign posters;
- organised correspondence to university leadership.

### Bowen Street Friday prayers, February 2008 – September 2009 — Grade B

Hundreds of Muslim men regularly prayed in Bowen Street as a visible part of
the campaign; women also participated in the boycott.

*Conflict note:* later secondary scholarship sometimes summarises the
street-prayer period differently. Preserve the differences rather than silently
harmonising them.

### Human Rights and Equal Opportunity Commission, 4 July 2008 — Grade A (for what HREOC said)

HREOC's official report described the RMIT prayer-facilities example in a
discussion of structural discrimination, and said such scenarios could have
wider institutional, social and economic impacts.

*Source:* https://humanrights.gov.au/sites/default/files/content/pdf/partnerships/combating_defamation_of_religions.pdf

**Critical qualification:** footnote 14 of the report cites a Wikipedia page
about Margaret Gardner as support for its RMIT example. The source is therefore
authoritative evidence of HREOC's public characterisation, but should **not**
automatically be treated as primary proof of every underlying allegation.

The same report also records a Freedom of Religion and Belief project involving
the Australian Multicultural Foundation in association with RMIT University,
Monash University and other organisations.

### Support network — Grade B

Ward & Wood record support from: Mufti Fehmi Naji el-Imam; the Australian
National Imams Council; the Islamic Society of Victoria; the Muslim Student
Association of Victoria; the Federation of Muslim Students and Youth; the
Islamic Council of Victoria; the Australian Union of Jewish Students; the
Christian Student Union; the National Union of Students; the RMIT NTEU branch;
and other campaign participants.

*Follow-up:* recover each organisation's own primary statement or resolution.

## 9. The modern era

### 2022 Eid Street Festival — Grade A

RMIT's official coverage identifies **Ayana Lokhandwala** as president of the
RUSU Islamic Society during the 2022 Eid Street Festival on Bowen Street, and
records Muslim student experiences of belonging and community.

*Source:* https://www.rmit.edu.au/students/news/2022/july/rmits-inclusive-community-on-show-during-eid-al-adha-celebrations

### 2023 — Multifaith and Wellbeing Centre

RMIT opened the Multifaith and Wellbeing Centre (Building 47) in 2023, part of
the long history of Muslim prayer facilities at RMIT.

**Do not state that the 2009 campaign directly caused the 2023 facility** unless
evidence establishing that causal chain is recovered.

### 2024 — The ISR rebrand — Grade A

RMITIS publicly announced the transition to **Islamic Society of RMIT (ISR)**.

*Source:* https://www.instagram.com/reel/C8ouD_zPybY/

This is a rebrand and a new organisational chapter — **not** evidence that
Muslim student organisation at RMIT began in 2024.

### Current information discrepancy — Building 47 access

RMIT's official Multifaith and Wellbeing Centre page states that the facility
is not a public prayer room and members of the public are not permitted to use
it
(https://www.rmit.edu.au/about/our-locations-and-facilities/facilities/prayer-rooms/multifaith-wellbeing-centre).

ISR leadership has separately confirmed that non-RMIT people can also attend.

**Status: unresolved current-operations conflict.** Public website copy should
avoid either stronger access claim and use neutral wording until clarified in
writing by the responsible RMIT facility or chaplaincy contact.

## 11. Places

### City — former Building 9 prayer facility

Building 9, Level 4. Recorded in a historical IslamicFinder listing dated 2005
and in the Deakin Islamic Directory 2007 ("RMIT, Corner Franklin & Bowen
Street, Building 9 Level 4, Melbourne 3000" — Grade C). Later described as
having existed for almost fifteen years before demolition in late 2007.

*To research:* exact establishment date, room number, floor plan, photographs,
brothers/sisters configuration, wudu facilities, management arrangement,
relationship with RMITIS, exact demolition date.

### Building 11 / Spiritual Centre

Central to the 2007–09 dispute.

*To research:* original approved plans, exact rooms, design drawings,
Arabic/Qur'anic decorative elements, access rules, booking policy, opening
date, and correspondence explaining the change to multifaith status.

### The Building 9 vs Building 11 discrepancy — P0

An older Salaam Monash handbook (containing a campus map dated October 2005 and
a link referring to "2006handbooks") lists RMIT City Campus prayer facilities
as **Building 11, Basement**, while the Deakin Islamic Directory 2007 gives
**Building 9, Level 4**, and the later campaign research describes the
long-standing Building 9 Level 4 facility with Building 11 as the replacement
arrangement.

Possible explanations: Salaam Monash contained incorrect RMIT information;
Building 11 was already in use alongside Building 9; Building 11 was a
temporary or additional facility; the handbook was partly updated after its
maps were produced; different publications used "prayer room" to mean different
spaces; or the currently understood chronology is incomplete.

**DO NOT SILENTLY RECONCILE.** Required evidence: original RMIT facilities
records and dated Muslim student guides.

### Bowen Street

Multiple historical roles: major City campus thoroughfare; Friday prayer
protest location during 2008–09; site of the 23 March 2009 rally; later listed
as the City Jumu'ah location; and the modern Eid Street Festival location.
*To research:* build a year-by-year use timeline.

### Kaleide Theatre

28 April 2009 — RMITIS public forum responding to RMIT's prayer-room claims.
*To research:* recover the event notice, recording, transcript, slides, photos.

### Building 108 / Bourke Street

RMIT's 1997 Annual Report records a major Building 108 renovation/opening in
1997. Later Muslim directories record Building 108, Level 3, Room 221 (male)
and Room 222 (female), with a Bourke Street Jumu'ah for brothers and sisters.
*To research:* determine when Muslim prayer use began.

### Tivoli

The 2009 campaign account discusses a Tivoli space in disputing RMIT's count of
Muslim prayer rooms. *To research:* identify the exact building, address, room
and usage period.

### Bundoora West — Building 202

*To investigate:* the 202.04.29 male prayer room; the sisters' prayer space;
Level 3 Jumu'ah; a 2016 return/move into the old male prayer room; and the
relationship to RMIT Bundoora Islamic Society.

### Bundoora East — Building 254

Current dedicated prayer-space location. *To research:* when established, what
preceded it, and how East/West responsibilities evolved.

### Brunswick — Building 514

Current prayer-space location. *To research:* the earliest Muslim prayer
arrangements at Brunswick, whether dedicated or multifaith spaces preceded
B514, and why no regular Brunswick Jumu'ah developed.

### Building 47 — Multifaith and Wellbeing Centre

Opened 2023. Current dedicated Islamic prayer rooms: male 47.02.02, female
47.01.07. *To research:* planning process, student consultation, RMITIS
participation, relationship (if any) to prior prayer-space history,
opening-event records, capital project documentation. Do **not** state that the

6. **What did Salaam RMIT contain?**

### Interview guidance

Ask open questions first — "What do you remember about when the Society
began?", "What existed when you arrived?", "Who was already involved?", "What
documents did you inherit?"

For Rini Akmeliawati specifically (at RMIT February 1993 – March 1997): what
Muslim student organisation existed in 1993, what it was called, whether there
was already a committee, where students prayed, whether Building 9 was already
in use, who the senior students and community organisers were, whether she
encountered United Muslim University Students, when she recalls a formal
Islamic Society beginning, whether newsletters or handbooks were produced, and
whether she retains documents, photographs or contacts.

**Do not ask** "Did you found ISR in 1993?" — that improperly assumes the
conclusion.
